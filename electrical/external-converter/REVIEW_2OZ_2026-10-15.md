## Return Current

J1.2, J2.1, J40.2, J41.2 and D2.1 each have four complete 0.50 mm thermal
spokes across 0.50 mm gaps. The refilled bottom plane contains continuous
conservative 5 mm corridors through the lower and right-side return paths.
No single small via or long narrow choke carries the complete return current.

At 10 A, 70 um copper and copper resistivity evaluated at 60 C:

| Screened element | Resistive loss |
|---|---:|
| Four equal thermal spokes at one power pad | 0.0071 W |
| Only one spoke carrying all 10 A, sensitivity case | 0.0285 W |
| Input positive tracks, 88.91 mm x 5 mm | 0.507 W |
| Illustrative input return, 65 mm x 5 mm | 0.371 W |
| Output positive tracks, 21.87 mm x 5 mm | 0.125 W |
| Illustrative output return, 34 mm x 5 mm | 0.194 W |
| Each 2 mOhm shunt | 0.200 W |
| F1, nominal cold resistance | 0.230 W |

With two pad thermals, the illustrative input subtotal is 1.32 W / 0.132 V;
output is 0.53 W / 0.053 V. These are NOT worst-case thermal bounds. Contacts,
barrels, spreading, fuse heating, wires, bridge and converter add losses.
At 56 um copper and 20% narrower spokes, the four-spoke loss is 0.0111 W.
The 56 um and width reductions are assumed sensitivities, not fabrication specs.

The plane and external converter may share input/output negatives. These are
not isolated circuits, and the external harness can redistribute return current.
Bridge cooling and stepped-current tests remain necessary; keep the current
thermal connections rather than making every power pad solid by default.

## Fuse Boundary

F1 remains the selected 0456020.ER, 20 A. Its datasheet specifies at least four
hours without opening at rated current and up to 60 seconds to open at 40 A.
The nominal 18 A2s is melting energy under the stated test, not a guaranteed
maximum total clearing energy. The manufacturer's reference PCB is 100 um /
10 mm; our 70 um / 5 mm mounting needs thermal qualification.
[Littelfuse 456 datasheet, revision 18 September 2025](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/littelfuse_fuse_456_datasheet.pdf?assetguid=d86b18f9-14fa-4764-87ff-8aec12e9a89d).

Therefore F1 is not a 10 A limiter, and this review does not establish fire
protection with arbitrary batteries. No fuse was changed without coordination.

Before an energy-rich source is supported, record source voltage, prospective
fault current, wire section and source fuse/limit; check its clearing envelope
against board and cable. A receiver battery still requires a fuse near itself:
its fault current does not pass through F1. Reversed J1 can feed the populated
bridge upstream of F1, so source-side protection remains necessary in that case.
